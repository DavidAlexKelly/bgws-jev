# ATGM hit chance and reliability, and company attack doctrine: leads, not yet read

From a web search on 21 November 2026. Every item was seen **only in
search snippets**: dote.osd.mil, globalsecurity.org and archive.org refused
this environment. **No value is changed on them.** Download the documents
into `docs/papers/pdfs/` to have them read and applied.

## `direct.guided.ph` (now 0.8, range 0.3–0.99)

- **DOT&E FY2017 Javelin report:** 22 flight tests gave "18 successful hits
  against vehicles, 2 successful hits against structure targets, and 1
  near miss and 1 complete miss against an IED team in the open". That is
  20/22 = 0.91, and 18/18 against vehicles.
  (dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2017/army/2017javelin.pdf)
- **DOT&E FY2016:** "six successful hits and one miss" in 7 flights.
- **DOT&E FY1998 (globalsecurity mirror):** "51 targets were hit out of 54
  attempts and only one of the 54 attempts was a reliability failure".
  That is 0.94.
- **Lockheed Martin product card:** ">94% engagement success rate". A
  manufacturer's figure, not a combat tally.
- **Ukraine, early claim (National Interest):** "out of the first 112 such
  missiles fired, 100 struck their targets", 0.89, unverified. Forbes
  (Hambling, Nov 2024) argues the real rate is far lower.
- **Israel 2006 (Globes):** 50 tanks hit by ATGMs, 22 penetrated. No shot
  count, so no hit rate can be derived.
- **Would mean:** about 0.9 in tests, lower in combat. The current 0.8
  sits between.

## `sim.fire.missile_fail_p` (now 0.06)

- **DOT&E FY97/98 Javelin:** missile reliability "to be 0.82" at Milestone
  III and "0.92" at system maturity. A point estimate of 0.988 from the
  1998 firings.
- **DOT&E FY2001 TOW:** reliability over the 10-year shelf life flagged.
- **Would mean:** a failure rate of about 0.01–0.08. The current 0.06 is
  within it.

## Company attack (`decide.heuristic.frontage` 300 m; support by fire)

- **FM 71-1 (company team):** inside a task force attack, "other company
  teams … will normally have the responsibility to suppress the enemy on
  the objective area from designated support by fire positions".
  Otherwise the company provides its own.
- **FM 3-90, ch. 5:** "two platoons may suppress while one platoon seizes
  a portion of the company objective". So doctrine allows both 1 SBF + 2
  assault and 2 SBF + 1 assault.
- **FM 3-21.8:** elements "should not generally get separated by more than
  300 meters". This supports the 300 m spacing as a maximum.
- **FM 17-15, ch. 3:** tanks keep "an interval of at least 100 meters".
- **Not found:** a doctrinal support-by-fire standoff distance from the
  objective.
