Real-time command design

You can pause the simulation at any point and have Claude write new orders; Claude runs at no other time. Jev is each unit's leader, making the call at a handful of well-defined moments within those orders. The game resolves everything else, including what each side can and cannot know.

1. Who decides what

Your instinct is right: Jev should not run the battlefield. It is a fast, single-pass judge of one well-posed question. It is good at "given what this troop can see, what now?" and poor at "how should the battlegroup win?", which needs reasoning across the whole map. So the unit-goal layer I proposed goes. The plan and each unit's intent come from Claude's orders, and Claude runs only when you ask for it.

2. What each unit knows

Most of your questions ("did it see where the shot came from?", "has it spotted us?") are about knowledge, so knowledge is modelled first. Each unit holds two beliefs, both kept by game logic. Jev is only ever shown the unit's beliefs, never the truth.

About each enemy: how much this unit knows

UnawareNothing. Its absence is not evidence it is not there.
→
SuspectedA bearing only: fired on from the north-west, heard firing, or a friend's vague report.
→
LocatedA position, type unknown. Can be fired on at a penalty.
→
IdentifiedPosition and type: a tank troop, 4 vehicles. Full odds can be worked out.
→
LostOut of sight for 2 min: a last known position with its age.

Moves up by a spotting roll each second, a locate roll when fired on, or a friend's report after 15 s. Moves down when out of sight.

About itself: does it think the enemy knows it is there?

UnobservedNo sign it has been seen.
→
Possibly seenA cue from an enemy it can see: halted, turned towards it, went to ground, started moving its way.
→
Known seenFired on, or rounds landing close.

Cues come only from what the unit can observe, never from whether the enemy really spotted it. So an enemy that stops at a waypoint by coincidence produces the same cue as one that has spotted it. That ambiguity is the real one, and Jev has to weigh it.

3. The unit state machine

A unit is always in one activity, which game logic carries out every second. It leaves an activity only through an event, and an event that matters becomes a decision point for Jev. Each activity lists what ends it. New orders from Claude arrive only after a pause, through decision point D0.

4. The decision points

This is the complete list. Each has a fixed trigger, a fixed set of options (generated by code, so Jev can only pick something legal), and a defined follow-up by game logic. If an event matches none of them, Jev is not asked.

#Decision pointTriggered when (game logic)Options Jev picks fromWhat the game does next
D0 New orders arrive You resume after "Generate orders" changed this unit's order, and the unit is in contact. Out of contact, it switches at once with no call.
  • Order marked "now": break off at once · under covering fire · fire and move back
  • Order marked "when able": comply now · finish this fight first · break contact, then comply
 "Finish first" is time-boxed: until the target is destroyed or lost, or 2 min, whichever comes first. Then the order runs with no further call, so it can't drift.
D1 New contact The unit locates an enemy it did not know about, by its own spotting or a friend's report
  • Carry on with the order
  • Engage now
  • Hold and wait for a trigger (D4 lists the triggers)
  • Get a better shot: hull-down, closer or flank; code picks the spot
  • Observe and report, without firing
  • Pull back to friends
 Engage: acquire, fire, then the enemy's locate roll (§5). Hold: the trigger is watched each second. Move: route and go. Observe: its reports reach the side.
D2 Fired on, shooter located Incoming fire and this unit's locate roll succeeded
  • Return fire from here (the drill has already taken cover if any was close)
  • Back into hull-down, then return fire
  • Pull back out of its sight
  • Assault it
  • Hold, stay in cover, don't fire
 As for engage or move. Returning fire gives the shooter a locate roll on this unit.
D3 Fired on, shooter not located Incoming fire and the locate roll failed: it knows only a bearing
  • Search that bearing (hold still; spotting rate doubled for 30 s)
  • Move into cover away from the bearing
  • Pull back
  • Carry on with the order
 Search: another locate roll each second; if found, D2; after 30 s, asked again. Each later volley gives a fresh locate roll with better odds.
D4 Trigger met The trigger chosen at D1 is met. Triggers are offered as concrete choices:
  • "hit chance reaches 80% (about 1,500 m)"
  • "it shows its side"
  • "inside 800 m"
  • "it reaches the treeline"
  • Fire now
  • Wait for a closer trigger
  • Let it pass and stay hidden
A trigger can instead be chosen as "fire automatically when met". Then no call is needed, which is quicker and is how a pre-briefed ambush works.		 As for engage. Waiting sets the new trigger.
D5 Enemy behaviour changed While hidden (waiting, observing or holding), an enemy it can see halts, turns towards it, goes to ground or starts towards it. At most once per enemy every 30 s.
  • Fire first, while you still can
  • Keep holding (it may be a coincidence)
  • Relocate quietly
  • Pull back
 The unit's self-belief becomes "possibly seen", which Jev sees as a band. It is not told whether the enemy really saw it.
D6 After a volley After the first volley at a target, and after any volley that knocks a vehicle out
  • Keep firing: it hasn't located you yet
  • Fire and move: shoot, then change position before it finds you
  • Shift to another target
  • Stop firing and go quiet
  • Pull back
 Jev sees whether the enemy has reacted (returned fire, moved, gone to ground) and an estimate of how likely it is to have located you, as a band.
D7 Target lost The target goes out of sight or is destroyed
  • Watch its last known position
  • Move to regain sight
  • Next target
  • Back to the order
 As chosen.
D8 Fire not working Engaging for 3 min with nothing knocked out
  • Get a better shot
  • Shift target
  • Stop firing and go quiet
  • Pull back
  • Keep at it
 As chosen.
D9 Friend needs help A friendly unit within 1.5 km is fired on or loses a vehicle, and this unit can see or reach its attacker

  • Move to support it
  • Carry on
 As chosen. This is how units cover each other without a commander.
D10 Order step done or blocked Arrived, route blocked, or the order's current phase is complete

  • Hold here
  • Overwatch

D11 Rallied A shaken or broken unit recovers
  • Rejoin the order
  • Hold and recover
  • Join the nearest friends
 Back into the loop.

What Jev is shown at every decision point:

  • the order's intent, urgency and engagement criteria;
  • the unit's beliefs about each enemy and about itself;
  • the odds, in words ("likely to knock one out within a minute", "its rounds can't get through your front");
  • its friends within reach;
  • the last few decisions and what came of them.

5. Example: a shot, and the enemy's view

Your first example. Troop A spots troop B at 1,900 m and decides to engage. The key step is that B does not automatically know where the fire came from. Game logic rolls for it.

6. Example: holding for a better shot

Your second example. Troop A, hull-down and unseen, spots troop B approaching at 2,600 m and decides to wait for a better shot. "Close enough" is not a later Jev question. It is part of the hold decision: Jev picks a concrete trigger and game logic watches for it.

7. Pausing and new orders

You can pause at any moment. While paused the clock is stopped, and "Generate orders" asks Claude for a new plan: for one side or both, optionally with your own guidance ("hold the bridge; the northern troop is too exposed"). Claude sees what that side knows: its units' strength, cohesion and position, the enemy as that side has sighted it, the last few minutes of events, and each unit's current order and how far it has got. You review the orders, then resume. Claude does nothing while the clock runs.

Hard orders or loose goals? Mission orders, with an urgency

Neither extreme works. "Stop and obey at once" makes units turn their backs in the middle of a fight, which gets them killed; real armies don't order that way. A loose goal risks units never getting round to it, because Jev will always find a good local reason to keep fighting. So each order Claude writes has four parts:

  • Task and phases: "advance to the ridge, then overwatch the bridge". What game logic carries out.
  • Intent, in one line: "keep the bridge under fire so the infantry can cross". Jev reads it at every decision, so a unit that is fighting weighs its fight against what the plan needs.
  • Urgency: now or when able, per unit. The default is when able; now is for orders where the timing is the point, like "withdraw before the flank collapses".
  • Constraints, which game logic enforces and Jev never overrides: "don't cross the river", "fire only in self-defence", "stay south of the road". Plus the default actions on contact, which are what the rules fall back to when Jev is unsure.

Jev is told the balance in plain words at every decision: the intent comes first; keep the unit alive and able to carry it out; take a local opportunity only when it doesn't cost the intent. The time-box and the constraints make sure that balance can't be argued away.

8. Rules that keep it sane

  • Claude runs only when you pause and ask. The game flags things for you; it never calls Claude on its own.
  • Only the decision points in §4 ask Jev. Everything else is game logic. That keeps calls to a few per unit per engagement.
  • One decision in flight per unit. Events that arrive while it's pending are folded into the next question, not queued as separate ones.
  • Timing: events are held 3 s to fold a burst into one question, answers take effect after the crew's reaction time (5–15 s by quality), and serious events skip the 20 s cooldown.
  • Cues are rationed: at most one D5 per enemy every 30 s, and only while the unit is trying to stay hidden.
  • Words, not numbers: odds and distances shown to Jev as bands, with the exact figures kept in the console log.
  • Short, fixed option lists per decision point, always in the same order, always including a safe default, and each with a rules fallback that follows the order's actions on contact.
  • The clock still waits for a late answer, so every game can be replayed exactly from its seed and its answers.

9. Build order

  1. Knowledge. Suspected, located, identified and lost, for each unit against each enemy. A locate roll when fired on, replacing today's automatic muzzle-flash reveal. The self-belief with its cues. Everything after this depends on it.
  2. Activities and decision points. The six activities, D1–D11 with their option generators and rules fallbacks, the trigger watcher, and "fire automatically when". This replaces today's catch-all options list.
  3. Pause and orders. Pause and resume with the clock stopped. "Generate orders" while paused, for one side or both, with your guidance. Orders with phases, intent, urgency, constraints and actions on contact. D0 on resume, and flags for you while running.
  4. Tuning. The locate-roll and cue figures (declared, like the rest), balance runs, and a small set of hand-checked situations for each decision point, to set Jev's confidence threshold properly.

What stays as it is: the combat model (hits, penetration, vehicles, hull-down), the movement modes, morale and rally, the drill, the replayable clock, and the turn game.
