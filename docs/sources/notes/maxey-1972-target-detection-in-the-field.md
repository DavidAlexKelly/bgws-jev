# Maxey and Caviness (1972): target detection in the field

**Source:** Jeffery L. Maxey and James A. Caviness, *Target Detection in
the Field*, HumRRO Professional Paper 11-72, Human Resources Research
Organization, May 1972. Presented at the 79th Annual Meeting of the
American Psychological Association, Washington, D.C., September 1971.
DTIC/NTIS AD-743 158. Approved for public release.

**File:** `raw/sensing/maxey-1972-target-detection-in-the-field.pdf`
(8 PDF pages; a scan with an OCR text layer). Read on 23 November 2026.
The paper itself is printed pp. 1–4 (PDF 5–8).

## What it is

The four-page conference version of the experiment reported in full in
`caviness-1972-target-detection-and-range-estimation.md`: 90 men, a
camouflaged soldier moving at 100–300 m, three terrains, three speeds.
`leads/naked-eye-search.md` listed it as "the likeliest primary source
of field numbers". It holds the experiment's numbers in a shorter form.

## Figures (Table 1, p. 3 [7])

Here non-detections are **included**, counted at the full time the
target was exposed. So the means are larger than in the full report,
which averages only the detections.

| Condition | Mean (s) | SD (s) |
|---|---|---|
| Low complexity | 2.82 | 5.18 |
| Medium complexity | 7.52 | 10.81 |
| High complexity | 13.80 | 18.74 |
| 100 m | 2.62 | 2.61 |
| 200 m | 3.93 | 4.65 |
| 300 m | 17.59 | 19.73 |

| Slow run | 7.51 | 10.65 |
| Fast run | 3.66 | 4.96 |

- At high complexity and 300 m **no** observer detected the target, so
  only 24 of the 27 conditions produced a distribution (p. 2 [6]).
- Detection is **not** a constant-rate (negative exponential) process
  for men on foot. The paper rejects the model in 22 of 24 conditions at
  p < .10. That is unlike the tanks in Stollmack (1966), for which it
  held (p. 2 [6]).
- Interactions: speed matters most in thick cover, and range matters
  most in thick cover (p. 3 [7]).

## Use

This is the same experiment as Caviness et al. (1972). That note has
the comparison with the model and why nothing is changed. The one new
point here is the exponential model: the land engine's detection is a
constant rate, so its distribution of times will not match men on foot
in cover. Stollmack's tank result supports it for vehicles.

## Limits

Those of the full report: alerted observers, a 30° sector, single
camouflaged men, 100–300 m, and Fort Benning vegetation.
